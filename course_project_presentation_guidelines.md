### 4. Content Guidelines

Please structure your presentation as follows:

1. **Title slide:** Project title, group number, and names of all group members.
2. **Motivation and objective:** The scientific question addressed in the project.
3. **System and methodology:** Material, interatomic potential used with its exact source, ensemble, timestep, and equilibration protocol.
4. **Comparison with literature:** Explicit comparison of trends and values with published work, with citations, OVITO snapshots.
5. **Discussion and limitations:** Assumptions, sources of uncertainty, and possible reasons for discrepancies.
6. **Conclusions:** Direct answers to the project questions.
7. **References:** Including the original potential reference and the literature used for comparison.

> **Note:** Focus on your own results and their interpretation. Avoid lengthy generic background material.

---

### 5. Do's and Don'ts

#### Do's
* Rehearse and time your presentation (aim for 10–11 minutes).
* State the interatomic potential clearly, including the exact parameter file and its original publication.
* Show quantitative plots and clear OVITO snapshots.
* Compare your results with the literature explicitly, with citations.
* Discuss uncertainties, statistical averaging, and limitations briefly.
* Include all group member names on the title slide and ensure every member can answer questions.
* Keep a few backup slides ready for Q&A.
* Answer Q&A concisely and acknowledge limitations honestly.

#### Don'ts
* Do not exceed the 12-minute hard stop.
* Do not read directly from the slides.
* Do not overcrowd slides with small text or too many plots.
* Do not omit the potential source or present results from an unvalidated or merged parameter file as validated.
* Do not claim agreement with the literature without a citation.
* Do not spend excessive time on generic background.
* Do not let a single member dominate the presentation or the Q&A.
* Do not rely on internet access during the presentation; use a local PDF backup.
* Do not argue with the audience during Q&A; respond professionally and briefly.
